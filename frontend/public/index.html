    Major Multiplier #1

    The Tobacco Surcharge

    Smoking is the single most influential driver in the entire Kaggle dataset. It accounts for over 60% of total variance, adding an average baseline surcharge of $15,000–$25,000 annually.

    Tobacco usage shifts policyholders immediately into High Risk classification.
    Major Multiplier #2

    Obese-Smoker Interaction (BMI ≥ 30)

    Our machine learning models capture the severe non-linear interaction effect between clinical obesity and active smoking. When both co-exist, predicted charges surge past $35,000/year.

    Compound synergy accounts for the highest residual spikes in tree algorithms.
    Linear Driver #3

    Age Aging Progression

    Charges increase roughly linearly by ~$250–$300 per additional year of age for non-smokers, driven by natural chronic illness probabilities and outpatient visit frequencies.

    Standard 3:1 age band restrictions apply in individual ACA markets.

    Model Evaluation Formulations & Methodology

    Mean Absolute Error (MAE):

    MAE = (1/n) Σ |y_i - ŷ_i| = $2,414.12

    Average absolute dollar deviation per applicant prediction.

    Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE):

    RMSE = √((1/n) Σ (y_i - ŷ_i)²) = $4,234.58

    Penalizes large outlier discrepancies quadratically.

    Coefficient of Determination (R²):

    R² = 1 - (Σ (y_i - ŷ_i)² / Σ (y_i - &ybar;)²) = 0.9024

    Over 90.2% of healthcare expense variance is explained by the model.
